# SOL-C Amendment 6 spacing decisions (2026-10-16)

This note supersedes the no-value dispositions in the spacing part of Amendment 5 only. These are research model contracts, not installed producers or experimental validation. The new maintainer rule permits disclosed extrapolation and alloy-family transfer. Empirical solidification correlations below are physics models of dendrite scale, not arbitrary numeric defaults. A value does not prove that dendrites or secondary arms actually formed.

## Common contract

Use each admitted cooling-side liquidus-front tuple before aggregation. Let `q=G*R` in K/s and R in m/s. Never substitute scan velocity for R, take absolute values of melting-side rates, or evaluate a law at separately aggregated G and R. Preserve A5 front orientation, co-location, transport-parent, positive/nonfinite, missing-front and endpoint-provenance checks. Missing chemistry may use a visibly named nominal-alloy surrogate below, never pretend it is an assay. Missing q means: "Provide a cooling-side thermal gradient and interface speed to compute spacing." Al SDAS also needs R: "Provide a cooling-side interface speed to compute secondary spacing."

All new process/family-transfer routes reach Tier 2 **Physics estimate** by default, at two significant figures, with no claimed interval. Their model/transfer uncertainty is unknown. Input intervals alone do not authenticate the correlations. A later Tier 1 **Estimated** arm may propagate the declared A6 thermal OFAT sweep through these same local laws, and must name the actual input minima/maxima and distinguish its input-sensitivity interval from unquantified model/transfer error. This note does not invent a specimen error range or claim that two different alloys bracket it. No interval is claimed for the default Tier 2 routes, so there are no hidden interval input ranges. No predeclared numerical coefficient ranges are used for the Tier 2 routes; coefficients remain the source values. The Ghosh original-support arm retains A5 conditional-input-band construction, eligible for Tier 1 only when the complete physically declared input sweep stays on the original support, while its off-support theoretical extrapolation is Tier 2. A5 numerical error alone is insufficient for that promotion; practical finite-G sweeps normally leave the fixed line and remain Tier 2.

For scalar `lambda=A*q^-n`, an input box gives `[A*q_hi^-n,A*q_lo^-n]`; Ti also varies the linear Al coefficient, so the lower endpoint uses Al_hi and q_hi. Al SDAS uses R_hi for its lower endpoint. Joint arms preserve correlated G/R and aggregate the local outputs within each arm first. Do not turn spatial percentiles, two chemistry alternatives or two models into error bars. If an SDAS law gives SDAS greater than PDAS, retain the two numerical model outputs with a disagreement warning rather than silently clipping them; they are model-specific length scales with different source calibrations.

An out-of-domain warning does not by itself erase a finite Tier 2 extrapolation. Mathematical impossibility, melting side, nonpositive inputs, missing essential parents, or a demonstrated physically incompatible structure does. If morphology says planar or absolute stability under the very binary model used, do not report its dendrite spacing. Say: "This model predicts a planar front. A cellular or planar stability and spacing model is needed." Secondary spacing is conditional on sidebranches being present, never a claim of observed SDAS or proof of branching. For a confirmed unbranched structure say: "Secondary arms are absent in this structure. A branching model or a measured branched region is needed for secondary arm spacing."

## Primary alloys

**Ti64, PDAS and SDAS: Tier 2.** [Mizukami et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.2355/isijinternational.ISIJINT-2020-089), HTML sections 4.3, 4.6 and 4.7, Eqs.4 and 5: `lambda1=(-62*CAl+699)*q^-0.39`, `lambda2=(-33*CAl+366)*q^-0.40`, both in micrometres; CAl is Al mass percent, q is liquidus-passage cooling rate. Use the entered Al value, or explicitly `CAl=6` nominal Ti64 if chemistry is absent. The source is EB-remelted titanium-alloy ingots. Fig.11 shows composition groups 2.9, 4.0, 4.3, 5.0 and 5.7 mass percent Al and a cooling-rate axis 1 to 100 K/s; those axis bounds are not a validated rectangle. Therefore always flag LPBF process transfer, flag unrepresented chemistry, and flag rates outside that plotted range. Refuse nonpositive linear coefficients, rather than floor them. Show: "Physics estimate from a titanium ingot solidification correlation. This is a process and composition transfer. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy." Append: "Inputs are outside the source conditions." when applicable. Neither output is alpha-lath width or final beta-grain size.

**AlSi10Mg, PDAS and SDAS: Tier 2.** [Gandolfi et al. 2021, Metals 11,1019](https://doi.org/10.3390/met11071019), PDF6 liquidus-rate definition/Fig.3, PDF11 Fig.9, PDF13 conclusions Eqs.1 and 2: `lambda1=165*q^-0.4` micrometres; `lambda2=22*(1000*R)^(-2/3)` micrometres for Al-10Si-0.45Mg. The second documented chemistry, Al-10Si-1Mg, uses `lambda2=14*(1000*R)^-1`; it is a named alternative only, not a parameter interval or default interpolation. The source axial liquidus-passage speed equals normal front speed only in its one-dimensional directional geometry. Using local normal R on a curved LPBF front is an additional observable/projection transfer and always warns; do not substitute scan speed. Use 0.45Mg as a declared source-alloy surrogate for nominal AlSi10Mg. This is actual PDAS, not renamed cellular size. Directional casting and the source's plotted rates differ from LPBF: Fig.3 spans q 0.05 to 10.75 K/s and R 0.300 to 1.240 mm/s across its two alloys; these are plotted support, not a validated multidimensional box. Always warn for LPBF transfer, chemistry mismatch, or outside that plotted support. Show: "Physics estimate from directional solidification of an Al-Si-Mg alloy. The source chemistry is Al-10Si-0.45Mg. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy." Append the common outside-source text when applicable.

## Other alloys

**316L, PDAS and SDAS: Tier 2.** [Bunaziv et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00170-023-11567-y), PDF16/printed926 Eq.2 and PDF17/printed927 Eq.6, micrometres: `lambda1=80*q^-0.33`, `lambda2=25*q^-0.28`. This primary 316L cladding study applies inherited austenitic-steel correlations; the originals cited there were not newly read. Its comparison uses cooling through 1450 to 1350 C, unlike the local liquidus rate here. Flag that observable transfer and LPBF-process transfer for every value. The study's illustrative inferred rates, approximately 115 to 5204 K/s, are not an independently established validity domain; flag q outside that span too. Do not interpret that span as calibrated bounds or measured machine rates. Show: "Physics estimate from an austenitic stainless steel spacing correlation used for 316L cladding. The cooling-rate definition and process are transferred. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."

**IN718 and IN625 PDAS:** retain the separately named Hunt and Kurz-Fisher Ni-5mass-percent-Nb alternatives of [Ghosh et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.06669), Table2/PDF8, Eqs.7/PDF12 and 8/PDF13, verified in [A5 spacing sources](sol_c_spacing_sources.md). Tier 1 on the original joint support with a declared positive input band; Tier 2 outside the fixed-G/x/cooling support with an explicit binary/alloy/process extrapolation warning. No snapping or source-domain flag reset. Use equation-derived A values `1.1773863284676703e-3` and `2.5821435943712695e-3` in `lambda=A*G^-0.5*R^-0.25` metres. For the extrapolation arm retain `R>G*Dl/DeltaT0` and `R<DeltaT0*Dl/(ke*Gamma)=0.976027397260274 m/s` as the binary dendrite-stability restrictions; the A5 rounded 0.98 remains a historical mask, not the exact absolute-stability result. A violated physical restriction refuses this dendrite law. Show: "Physics estimate from two binary Ni-Nb dendrite models transferred to [IN718/IN625]. Inputs are outside the source study where indicated. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."

**IN718 and IN625 SDAS: Tier 2.** [Mitchell 2005, The Precipitation of Primary Carbides in IN718 and its Relation to Solidification Conditions](https://www.tms.org/superalloys/10.7449/2005/Superalloys_2005_299_310.pdf), PDF4/printed302 Eq.1: `lambda2=19.5*q^-0.63` micrometres. This is an ingot/remelting correlation, not an LPBF-calibrated equation; no fitted-rate domain or regression uncertainty is given beside Eq.1. Treat every LPBF use as extrapolation. IN625 additionally transfers a Ni-alloy-family law from IN718. No claim that these spacings necessarily exist after rapid cellular solidification; tiny extrapolated scales deserve the same warning, never a manufactured numerical floor. Show: "Physics estimate of secondary dendrite scale from an IN718 ingot correlation [transferred to IN625]. Inputs are outside the source process. Secondary arms may not form. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."
