This carries forward SOL-C Amendments 5-7 in [SPEC_solidification_2026-10-14.md](SPEC_solidification_2026-10-14.md), with the maintainer's **current** Tier 2 name `Uncalibrated estimate` taking precedence over the historical `Physics estimate` label. 1. **Tier 1: Estimated.** Every input, property/model parameter, history, geometry, derivative and downstream mapping in the physical dependency closure must be source-supported in the actual alloy, process, state and domain. Execute and retain the stated input perturbations with fresh solves and geometry/front extraction. Display `input sensitivity range`, never an error bar, confidence interval or accuracy claim. Record input values/bounds/units/evidence, nominal and successful/failed arm IDs, numerical refinement, limits and omitted components. A planned range or a successful synthetic checker is not an executed actual-input range.
2. **Tier 2: Uncalibrated estimate.** Any family, process, observable or source-domain transfer anywhere in the dependency closure forces Tier 2. Show reduced precision, normally one or two significant figures, the named assumptions/violations and the exact sentence: **"Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."** Internal arithmetic retains full precision. Optional parameter scenarios are auxiliary sensitivity, never a promotion or a disguised uncertainty interval.
3. **No value.** Refuse a mathematically/physically undefined model branch, missing necessary input, incompatible phase model, failed conservation/refinement or a falsified closure. Give a quantity-specific constructive sentence naming the needed input/model. Software bugs must be fixed separately; they are not evidence that an unspecified physics model exists. Physically meaningful finite extrapolation may be Tier 2 with warnings; singularities, missing states and failed conservation are not extrapolation.
4. **Evidence kinds.** Distinguish measured/user observations, literature values, fitted/calibrated coefficients, calculated parents, assumed scenarios and manufactured verification fixtures. These are scientific descriptions; map them to the existing registry vocabulary in a coordinated typed migration instead of inventing wire codes. Record source equation/table/page, units, material/process state, database/model version, source hashes where available and input IDs. Retain complete dependency closure and stale-input invalidation. Defaults are assumed inputs until their evidence is established.
5. **Observable identity.** Do not put constrained reaction stress under an unqualified whole-part residual field, a beam displacement under the old undefined distortion index, Gumbel statistics under measured CT evidence, equilibrium fractions under retained rapid-cooling phases, scalar wall-temperature scenarios under spatial maxima, or ray geometry under a solved cavity prediction. New observables require distinct producer/validator/consumer definitions. Existing evidence tiers and experimental-validation flags remain independent of the new estimate labels.
6. **Parent integrity.** No Tier 2 result repairs an invalid parent. No zero substituted for unknown geometry/defects/fractions; no copying a different alloy without an explicit sourced model and declared transfer. No generic yield strength, hardness, optical tuple, CET nucleation density or calibrated coefficient silently inserted. `not-requested`, absent phase, `not-applicable`, a pending asynchronous response and descriptive metadata are not missing numeric physics.
7. **Visible language.** Plain English, no em dash, no accuracy claims. General value template: "Uncalibrated estimate: about {value} {unit} from {named model} for {observable}. {specific assumptions and domain warning} Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy." Missing-model text explains the next computable action. Measured metadata is displayed as measured metadata, without an estimate tier.

## Group 1. Transient thermal history, peak temperature and melt envelope

**Implementation priority 1. Best attainable release tier is Tier 2 for every alloy with the current property/optical dependency chains.** This is a proposed physics contract, not an installed solver or a successful rerun of the four stopped presets. A failed run has no completed temperature history, final peak or final melt geometry to relabel as an estimate.

### G1.1 Separate the existing stop, the usable prefix and the missing model

`python/lpbf_simulation.py` is a fixed-material-domain enthalpy-conduction solver. Its boiling-limit stop is intentional model admission, not a numerical defect. SOL-T already translates the worker refusal to `THERMAL_BOILING_LIMIT`. Optional `evaporationModel=True` is explicitly a boiling cap: it holds excess enthalpy in the cell, clips the temperature, and multiplies liquid conductivity by an uncited factor. It exports neither a mass flux nor a latent-heat sink. Enabling that flag, removing the stop, or changing the boiling constant until a default succeeds does not close evaporation.

The cheap first release is **partial-result plumbing**: retain only accepted pre-stop timesteps, the accepted end time, the first rejected step and its reason. A valid prefix is a Tier 2 temperature/history estimate over `[start, last accepted time]`; label its maximum **maximum over the computed interval**, never the final-process peak. Numerical fields from the rejected step must not enter that result. This recovers useful process information without changing the existing physical domain. Returning the valid prefix is a software lifecycle feature; the missing remainder is a physics gap. A nonfinite/failed accepted step is not recoverable by a tier label.

### G1.2 A reduced evaporation model that can actually produce an estimate

Adopt a **new, separately named transient conduction model with evaporative energy loss**, not the existing cap. The source basis is [Gan2021 supplementary Methods 1, PDF pp.2-4, Eqs.1-25](https://static-content.springer-cdn.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-021-22704-0/MediaObjects/41467_2021_22704_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), particularly enthalpy Eqs.15-20; [Fabbro2020 Appendix A, PDF pp.18-20, Eqs.A1-A12](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10041487) locates the outgoing melt/vapor energy terms and the high-evaporation flux relation. Gan's multiphase method includes flow, interface motion and vapor transport. Dropping those equations produces a **declared reduced model**, not a reproduction of the full source method.

For an energy-only fixed-surface screening arm solve

`rho dh/dt = div(k grad T) + q_volume`, with `h(T)=integral(cp(T)dT)+Lf f_liquid(T)` and `q_into_material=A I - epsilon sigmaSB(T^4-Tgas^4)-hc(T-Tgas)-j Lv` at the exposed boundary. Specify initial/substrate temperature, the actual beam trajectory and boundary contacts. Use either surface deposition or a normalized volumetric source, never both for the same incident power. `f_liquid` is a stated monotone solidus/liquidus interpolation, not a CALPHAD fraction.

For a single effective vapor species, the proposed high-intensity source convention is

`psat(T)=p_ref exp[(Lv M/Rg)(1/Tv_ref-1/T)]`, `j=(1-beta) psat sqrt[M/(2 pi Rg T)]`, and `pr=(1+beta) psat/2`.

`Tv_ref` must refer to `p_ref`; use the declared 1-atmosphere convention consistently. Fabbro uses an approximately `1e5 Pa` reference and `beta=0.2`; P8 uses `101325 Pa`. The source's condensation fraction is a high-evaporation **model assumption**, not measured accommodation for every alloy. The pure high-intensity formula is not automatically valid near atmospheric evaporation onset. Gan Eqs.12/14 use a transition between low- and high-intensity regimes; a new physical run must explicitly choose and document that transition or confine the high-intensity formula to its stated regime. Never set the ambient gas partial pressure equal to the total chamber pressure to invent a condensation rate.

The fixed-surface arm removes evaporative energy but holds material geometry and composition fixed. It may provide Tier 2 **temperature and isotherm-envelope estimates under that hypothesis**, with emitted mass explicitly diagnostic. It is not a conserved ablation calculation, and it cannot produce physical surface recession, material loss, cavity shape or actual keyhole-wall maxima. For those quantities implement conservative mass removal/recession, or the full Gan one-fluid/VOF closure. In a moving control volume the removed energy is `j (h_liquid(T)+Lv)`; latent flux and advected liquid enthalpy must be counted exactly once. Include expelled liquid enthalpy and kinetic energy if liquid ejection is modelled. The checker exercises this distinction. Replacing P8's scalar flat HKL balance by the same scalar balance under a new name is prohibited by its known contradiction.

### M1. One-dimensional elastoplastic thermal cycle, Tier 2

Constitutive sources: A. F. Bower, *Applied Mechanics of Solids*, open author text [3.2.2](https://solidmechanics.org/text/Chapter3_2/Chapter3_2.htm) for thermoelastic strain and [3.7.2, 3.7.5-8](https://solidmechanics.org/text/Chapter3_7/Chapter3_7.htm) for additive elastic/plastic strain, yield and flow. The following scalar reduction and benchmark are derived here, not a fitted LPBF law. A same-alloy detailed alternative exists in Xiao et al., *Materials & Design* 193 (2020) 108846, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2020.108846), sequential thermal-structural model. Its existence does not fill this application's constitutive curves or boundary conditions.

State at step n: thermal strain `eps_th,n=integral[Tref,Tn] alpha(T)dT`, plastic strain `eps_p,n`, imposed total strain `eps_tot,n`, stress `sigma_n`. For a constant-E, ideal-plastic reduction, trial `s=E*(eps_tot,n-eps_th,n-eps_p,n-1)`; if `abs(s)<=sy`, keep `sigma=s, eps_p=eps_p,old`; otherwise `sigma=sign(s)*sy`, `eps_p=eps_tot-eps_th-sigma/E`. Units: E, sy, sigma in Pa; alpha in K^-1; strains dimensionless; temperature in K. This is a return mapping from the stated elastic/plastic law, not an empirical clipping rule on an unrelated thermal index. Temperature-dependent E, alpha and sy require interpolated source curves and a consistent return-mapping implementation, not ambient constants silently extended to melting.

For a completely fixed bar, initial stress/plastic strain zero at Tref, constant inputs and one heat/cool cycle `Tref -> Tref+dT -> Tref`, the final tensile stress is `min(sy,max(0,E*alpha*dT-sy))`. With no yielding the final residual is zero. Once the bar is freed at its return temperature, uniform axial stress is zero and the released length strain is its accumulated plastic strain. This last distinction is mandatory: the constrained reaction cannot be displayed as free-part self-equilibrated stress. Reheating, partial restraint and a specified stress-free reset temperature require the actual declared history and boundary law.

Inputs and evidence: T(t) and material-point location are **calculated** from the selected thermal parent or **measured** from a supplied history; use Tier 2 when that parent is transferred. `Tref`, initial stress/plastic strain, fully fixed constraint and perfect plasticity are **declared model/scenario assumptions**, never measured. E(T), alpha(T), sy(T) are **measured** user curves or **literature** curves with alloy, heat treatment, orientation, temperature and strain-rate coverage. Legacy unsourced wrought yield strengths are not sourced alloy defaults. No new alloy constants are adopted here. A user may explicitly enter scalar scenario values; store those as **assumed**, not material truth. A constant-input run states its constant-property approximation and range, even when its inputs came from one temperature.

### M2. Distortion from inherent strain and geometry, Tier 2

Model: solve `div(sigma)=0`, `sigma=C:(sym(grad u)-eps*)` with actual support/removal sequence and eigenstrain `eps*` from a declared calibrated source or an M1-derived scenario. This follows the elasticity equations above; the proposed one-dimensional beam reduction below is an explicit derived model, not an adopted commercial inherent-strain calibration. A Ti LPBF implementation example is [*Predictive Modeling and Contour Method Validation ... Ti6Al4V ... Inherent Strain Method*, 2026, methods 2.2](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/12/5986). It calibrates orthotropic inherent strain. Its calibration does not transfer silently to this machine or to AlSi10Mg. No numerical coefficient from that study is adopted.

Early low-cost route for Ti64, AlSi10Mg, 316L, IN718 and IN625: a rectangular released cantilever with length L, thickness H, constant E and known axial eigenstrain profile eps*(z). At the elastic centroid z=0, `eps_bar=(1/H) integral eps*(z) dz`, `kappa=(12/H^3) integral z eps*(z) dz`, `sigma(z)=E*(eps_bar+kappa*z-eps*(z))`, `tipDeflection=kappa*L^2/2`. These equations follow independently from zero axial force/moment and beam compatibility; the numbers 12 and 1/2 are geometry/integration factors, not alloy constants. Uniform eigenstrain produces axial shortening and zero curvature, not a fabricated bending displacement. Constant E is an explicit scalar approximation; a modulus profile requires E-weighted section integrals. The proposed new dimensionless observable is `abs(tipDeflection)/H`, explicitly named **tip deflection divided by thickness**. It must not silently populate the old unmodelled `distortionIndex` as though the observable were unchanged. Either migrate its schema/label to this defined observable or keep that legacy quantity unavailable and expose the new one.

Inputs: L, H, orientation, root attachment and release state from **measured/user geometry**, not a thermal-source width; eps*(z) from **calculated M1 history** or **measured/calibrated** matched-build data, or a visibly **assumed scenario profile**; E from the same declared material state. Existing STL triangles alone do not determine eps*, supports or release sequence. All five alloys remain Tier 2 for this geometry/model reduction; Ti transformation and Al relaxation warnings from M1 propagate. No Tier 1 sensitivity claim is made. Optional sensitivity runs are auxiliary scenarios and cannot remove transfers.

Domain: slender small-deflection beam with a rectangular section and declared eigenstrain, elastic release and one cantilever root. For nonbeam geometries request an elastic or elastoplastic structural solve using that geometry; do not force a beam formula into an arbitrary STL. Require a declared small-rotation acceptance threshold before running. Refuse missing L/H/profile/supports, nonpositive dimensions, nonfinite strain, large rotations, or release stresses inconsistent with the selected elastic constitutive domain. More advanced large-deflection/plastic release is a missing model, not a justification for a padded range. Recoater collision additionally needs layerwise upward displacement and actual recoater clearance; final tip displacement alone cannot classify it.

Visible value: "Uncalibrated estimate: about {deflection} mm tip displacement for the selected cantilever and inherent-strain profile. This is a released-beam scenario. The geometry, restraint and strain profile determine the result. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."

Visible refusal: "Distortion needs the part geometry, support and release conditions, and an inherent-strain field or a thermal mechanical history. A laser parameter vector alone does not determine displacement."
