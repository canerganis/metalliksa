## Group 1. Transient thermal history, peak temperature and melt envelope

**Implementation priority 1. Best attainable release tier is Tier 2 for every alloy with the current property/optical dependency chains.** This is a proposed physics contract, not an installed solver or a successful rerun of the four stopped presets. A failed run has no completed temperature history, final peak or final melt geometry to relabel as an estimate.

### G1.1 Separate the existing stop, the usable prefix and the missing model

`python/lpbf_simulation.py` is a fixed-material-domain enthalpy-conduction solver. Its boiling-limit stop is intentional model admission, not a numerical defect. SOL-T already translates the worker refusal to `THERMAL_BOILING_LIMIT`. Optional `evaporationModel=True` is explicitly a boiling cap: it holds excess enthalpy in the cell, clips the temperature, and multiplies liquid conductivity by an uncited factor. It exports neither a mass flux nor a latent-heat sink. Enabling that flag, removing the stop, or changing the boiling constant until a default succeeds does not close evaporation.

The cheap first release is **partial-result plumbing**: retain only accepted pre-stop timesteps, the accepted end time, the first rejected step and its reason. A valid prefix is a Tier 2 temperature/history estimate over `[start, last accepted time]`; label its maximum **maximum over the computed interval**, never the final-process peak. Numerical fields from the rejected step must not enter that result. This recovers useful process information without changing the existing physical domain. Returning the valid prefix is a software lifecycle feature; the missing remainder is a physics gap. A nonfinite/failed accepted step is not recoverable by a tier label.

### G1.2 A reduced evaporation model that can actually produce an estimate

Adopt a **new, separately named transient conduction model with evaporative energy loss**, not the existing cap. The source basis is [Gan2021 supplementary Methods 1, PDF pp.2-4, Eqs.1-25](https://static-content.springer-cdn.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-021-22704-0/MediaObjects/41467_2021_22704_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), particularly enthalpy Eqs.15-20; [Fabbro2020 Appendix A, PDF pp.18-20, Eqs.A1-A12](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10041487) locates the outgoing melt/vapor energy terms and the high-evaporation flux relation. Gan's multiphase method includes flow, interface motion and vapor transport. Dropping those equations produces a **declared reduced model**, not a reproduction of the full source method.

For an energy-only fixed-surface screening arm solve

`rho dh/dt = div(k grad T) + q_volume`, with `h(T)=integral(cp(T)dT)+Lf f_liquid(T)` and `q_into_material=A I - epsilon sigmaSB(T^4-Tgas^4)-hc(T-Tgas)-j Lv` at the exposed boundary. Specify initial/substrate temperature, the actual beam trajectory and boundary contacts. Use either surface deposition or a normalized volumetric source, never both for the same incident power. `f_liquid` is a stated monotone solidus/liquidus interpolation, not a CALPHAD fraction.

For a single effective vapor species, the proposed high-intensity source convention is

`psat(T)=p_ref exp[(Lv M/Rg)(1/Tv_ref-1/T)]`, `j=(1-beta) psat sqrt[M/(2 pi Rg T)]`, and `pr=(1+beta) psat/2`.

`Tv_ref` must refer to `p_ref`; use the declared 1-atmosphere convention consistently. Fabbro uses an approximately `1e5 Pa` reference and `beta=0.2`; P8 uses `101325 Pa`. The source's condensation fraction is a high-evaporation **model assumption**, not measured accommodation for every alloy. The pure high-intensity formula is not automatically valid near atmospheric evaporation onset. Gan Eqs.12/14 use a transition between low- and high-intensity regimes; a new physical run must explicitly choose and document that transition or confine the high-intensity formula to its stated regime. Never set the ambient gas partial pressure equal to the total chamber pressure to invent a condensation rate.

The fixed-surface arm removes evaporative energy but holds material geometry and composition fixed. It may provide Tier 2 **temperature and isotherm-envelope estimates under that hypothesis**, with emitted mass explicitly diagnostic. It is not a conserved ablation calculation, and it cannot produce physical surface recession, material loss, cavity shape or actual keyhole-wall maxima. For those quantities implement conservative mass removal/recession, or the full Gan one-fluid/VOF closure. In a moving control volume the removed energy is `j (h_liquid(T)+Lv)`; latent flux and advected liquid enthalpy must be counted exactly once. Include expelled liquid enthalpy and kinetic energy if liquid ejection is modelled. The checker exercises this distinction. Replacing P8's scalar flat HKL balance by the same scalar balance under a new name is prohibited by its known contradiction.

### G10.2 A usable new Tier 2 wall-state candidate, rather than relabelling the falsified balance

[Fabbro2020 Appendix A, PDF pp.18-20](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10041487) provides a **generalized piston model** with inclined half-cone, molten-film ejection and explicit mass/momentum/energy balances. It is materially different from the contradicted nonreceding flat local-HKL balance: expelled melt carries energy; surface inclination and illuminated area enter the calculation. It is a candidate for **conditional conical-wall temperature, recoil pressure, ejected-liquid velocity and cavity depth**. A source-process/geometry/optics transfer to LPBF makes it Tier 2 for all five alloys, even after every coefficient and sensitivity arm is supplied. It gives no spatial peak wall field and no transient history.

Let `theta` be front inclination, `d` beam diameter, `e=d cot(theta)`, `Vd=v cos(theta)`, `alpha_l=kl/(rho_l cpl)`, and

`Y(Tw)=ln[1+rho_l cpl(Tw-Tm)/(rho_s cps(Tm-T0)+rho_s Lf)]`, `delta0=alpha_l Y/Vd`, `Sv=(1.5 pi/4)d^2/sin(theta)`.

The last area is the source's approximated illuminated half-cone area; do not silently replace its numerical factor by another cone convention. Fabbro uses a single melting temperature `Tm`; for an alloy use the explicitly named effective-melting/liquidus convention and disclose this observable/model reduction.

`mdot_in=0.5 rho_s v(d+2 delta0)e`, `mdot_m=rho_l Um delta0 e`, `mdot_v=j Sv`, and `mdot_in=mdot_m+mdot_v` (A2).

`Um=sqrt[2(pr(Tw)-pamb)/rho_l]` (A10), with the same-temperature `psat,j,pr` from G1.2. Require `pr>pamb` and `Tw>Tm`; do not take an absolute pressure difference to create a branch. Elimination of A1/A2 gives an independent useful implementation identity:

`cos(theta)=[(rho_l Um/v-rho_s) alpha_l Y+j(1.5 pi/4)d]/(0.5 rho_s v d)`.

Require `0<cos(theta)<1`; then recover `e,delta0,Sv`. Energy closes through

`Af(theta) P = mdot_m Hm + mdot_v Hv + Pcond + mdot_m Um^2/2`,

`Hm=cps(Tm-T0)+Lf+cpl(Tw-Tm)/2`, `Hv=cps(Tm-T0)+Lf+cpl(Tw-Tm)+Lv`,

`Pcond=ks(Tm-T0){0.5 m0[v d/(2 alpha_s)](1+2 delta0/d)+n0}e`, `alpha_s=ks/(rho_s cps)`, with source approximate `m0=n0=2.3` (A8). Integrating temperature-dependent heat capacities instead of these constants is a separately declared extension, not the printed constant-property equation.

`Af` is unpolarized Fresnel absorptance at incidence `pi/2-theta`; the source example uses **steel** complex index `3.6+5i` at `1.06 micrometres`, including in its Ti example. If used for Ti64/Al/IN718/316L/IN625 it is an explicitly named optical proxy, not measured alloy optics. Prefer user/source alloy-state-wavelength optical inputs when available. All required thermophysical constants must refer to one named alloy/model tuple with the solid/liquid distinction preserved. Ti can use the complete, separately named Gan2021 source-model tuple in the checker. Al uses its own positive liquid/solid authority with a named pure-Al vapor proxy. IN718/316L use their own existing positive nominal property conventions plus explicit vapor tuple; an unlocated numerical coefficient stays a declared nominal assumption with unresolved evidence. IN625 can use its own G1.4 table plus a named compatible vapor triple; Table1 alone does not supply that triple. The existing P8 optional IN625 Raoult record remains an explicit alternative with its composition/species assumptions, never a silently completed nominal.

At prescribed `P,v,d`, enumerate physically admitted branches of `P(Tw)-P_request=0`, report branch multiplicity, and apply a declared continuation/stability rule. No bracket, an unphysical half-cone, nonpositive properties, unresolved optical power or failed mass/energy residual gives no value; return the missing assumption/branch reason. An unresolved unique-branch selection may show all identified model alternatives, not select the most convenient value. Source section3.1 restricts its half-cone first-incidence description to approximately `e/d<3`; Appendix A motivates its conduction approximation at high Pe and `e/d>0.5`. Outside that stated geometry or at low Pe a finite extrapolation is Tier 2 with the actual violated condition, never Tier 1. Strongly singular cone/zero normal speed is a physical/numerical refusal. There is no experimentally established universal P8 validity rectangle.

The supplied stdlib checker forms **90 finite Gan-Ti/steel-optics conical states** over a deterministic wall-temperature scan, with direct A2 mass and A3 energy residual checks, independent Fresnel identity and outgoing-enthalpy tests. It does **not** establish a selected root for any UI preset, prove stability, evaluate an Al/IN718/316L/IN625 branch, or validate a new served quantity. An implementer may ship this candidate only after those request-level solves and branch/domain checks pass.

Visible text: **"Uncalibrated estimate of the conditional conical-wall {quantity} for {alloy}. This model includes evaporative recoil and liquid ejection under a stationary half-cone assumption. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."** Add "The optical constants use the stated steel proxy" if applicable. No alias to `physicalWallPeakTemperature_K` is authorized.
