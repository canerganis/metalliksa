### M1. One-dimensional elastoplastic thermal cycle, Tier 2

Constitutive sources: A. F. Bower, *Applied Mechanics of Solids*, open author text [3.2.2](https://solidmechanics.org/text/Chapter3_2/Chapter3_2.htm) for thermoelastic strain and [3.7.2, 3.7.5-8](https://solidmechanics.org/text/Chapter3_7/Chapter3_7.htm) for additive elastic/plastic strain, yield and flow. The following scalar reduction and benchmark are derived here, not a fitted LPBF law. A same-alloy detailed alternative exists in Xiao et al., *Materials & Design* 193 (2020) 108846, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2020.108846), sequential thermal-structural model. Its existence does not fill this application's constitutive curves or boundary conditions.

State at step n: thermal strain `eps_th,n=integral[Tref,Tn] alpha(T)dT`, plastic strain `eps_p,n`, imposed total strain `eps_tot,n`, stress `sigma_n`. For a constant-E, ideal-plastic reduction, trial `s=E*(eps_tot,n-eps_th,n-eps_p,n-1)`; if `abs(s)<=sy`, keep `sigma=s, eps_p=eps_p,old`; otherwise `sigma=sign(s)*sy`, `eps_p=eps_tot-eps_th-sigma/E`. Units: E, sy, sigma in Pa; alpha in K^-1; strains dimensionless; temperature in K. This is a return mapping from the stated elastic/plastic law, not an empirical clipping rule on an unrelated thermal index. Temperature-dependent E, alpha and sy require interpolated source curves and a consistent return-mapping implementation, not ambient constants silently extended to melting.

For a completely fixed bar, initial stress/plastic strain zero at Tref, constant inputs and one heat/cool cycle `Tref -> Tref+dT -> Tref`, the final tensile stress is `min(sy,max(0,E*alpha*dT-sy))`. With no yielding the final residual is zero. Once the bar is freed at its return temperature, uniform axial stress is zero and the released length strain is its accumulated plastic strain. This last distinction is mandatory: the constrained reaction cannot be displayed as free-part self-equilibrated stress. Reheating, partial restraint and a specified stress-free reset temperature require the actual declared history and boundary law.

Inputs and evidence: T(t) and material-point location are **calculated** from the selected thermal parent or **measured** from a supplied history; use Tier 2 when that parent is transferred. `Tref`, initial stress/plastic strain, fully fixed constraint and perfect plasticity are **declared model/scenario assumptions**, never measured. E(T), alpha(T), sy(T) are **measured** user curves or **literature** curves with alloy, heat treatment, orientation, temperature and strain-rate coverage. Legacy unsourced wrought yield strengths are not sourced alloy defaults. No new alloy constants are adopted here. A user may explicitly enter scalar scenario values; store those as **assumed**, not material truth. A constant-input run states its constant-property approximation and range, even when its inputs came from one temperature.

### M2. Distortion from inherent strain and geometry, Tier 2

Model: solve `div(sigma)=0`, `sigma=C:(sym(grad u)-eps*)` with actual support/removal sequence and eigenstrain `eps*` from a declared calibrated source or an M1-derived scenario. This follows the elasticity equations above; the proposed one-dimensional beam reduction below is an explicit derived model, not an adopted commercial inherent-strain calibration. A Ti LPBF implementation example is [*Predictive Modeling and Contour Method Validation ... Ti6Al4V ... Inherent Strain Method*, 2026, methods 2.2](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/12/5986). It calibrates orthotropic inherent strain. Its calibration does not transfer silently to this machine or to AlSi10Mg. No numerical coefficient from that study is adopted.

Early low-cost route for Ti64, AlSi10Mg, 316L, IN718 and IN625: a rectangular released cantilever with length L, thickness H, constant E and known axial eigenstrain profile eps*(z). At the elastic centroid z=0, `eps_bar=(1/H) integral eps*(z) dz`, `kappa=(12/H^3) integral z eps*(z) dz`, `sigma(z)=E*(eps_bar+kappa*z-eps*(z))`, `tipDeflection=kappa*L^2/2`. These equations follow independently from zero axial force/moment and beam compatibility; the numbers 12 and 1/2 are geometry/integration factors, not alloy constants. Uniform eigenstrain produces axial shortening and zero curvature, not a fabricated bending displacement. Constant E is an explicit scalar approximation; a modulus profile requires E-weighted section integrals. The proposed new dimensionless observable is `abs(tipDeflection)/H`, explicitly named **tip deflection divided by thickness**. It must not silently populate the old unmodelled `distortionIndex` as though the observable were unchanged. Either migrate its schema/label to this defined observable or keep that legacy quantity unavailable and expose the new one.

Inputs: L, H, orientation, root attachment and release state from **measured/user geometry**, not a thermal-source width; eps*(z) from **calculated M1 history** or **measured/calibrated** matched-build data, or a visibly **assumed scenario profile**; E from the same declared material state. Existing STL triangles alone do not determine eps*, supports or release sequence. All five alloys remain Tier 2 for this geometry/model reduction; Ti transformation and Al relaxation warnings from M1 propagate. No Tier 1 sensitivity claim is made. Optional sensitivity runs are auxiliary scenarios and cannot remove transfers.

Domain: slender small-deflection beam with a rectangular section and declared eigenstrain, elastic release and one cantilever root. For nonbeam geometries request an elastic or elastoplastic structural solve using that geometry; do not force a beam formula into an arbitrary STL. Require a declared small-rotation acceptance threshold before running. Refuse missing L/H/profile/supports, nonpositive dimensions, nonfinite strain, large rotations, or release stresses inconsistent with the selected elastic constitutive domain. More advanced large-deflection/plastic release is a missing model, not a justification for a padded range. Recoater collision additionally needs layerwise upward displacement and actual recoater clearance; final tip displacement alone cannot classify it.

Visible value: "Uncalibrated estimate: about {deflection} mm tip displacement for the selected cantilever and inherent-strain profile. This is a released-beam scenario. The geometry, restraint and strain profile determine the result. Uncertainty is unknown and may be large. Calibrate to your machine for better accuracy."

Visible refusal: "Distortion needs the part geometry, support and release conditions, and an inherent-strain field or a thermal mechanical history. A laser parameter vector alone does not determine displacement."

### F1. Murakami defect-conditioned threshold amplitude, Tier 2

For supplied or explicitly assumed projected `s=sqrt(area)` in micrometres and Vickers HV, `sigma_w=C*(HV+120)/s^(1/6)` in MPa, with C=1.43 for a surface defect and 1.56 for an internal defect, fully reversed uniaxial loading R=-1. HV is the Vickers number, not hardness in MPa. Project area normal to the load direction. Primary open sources displaying the equation: [Uematsu et al. 2021, *Metals* 11, 964, Eq.1](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/11/6/964), surface form; [*Monotonic and Fatigue Behavior of EBM ... Ti-6Al-4V*, 2020, Eqs.10-11](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/13/20/4642), both geometries; [*Internal Crack Initiation ... Ti6Al4V*, 2021, Eq.1](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1944/14/18/5315), internal form, [open PMC mirror](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8468648/). Those Ti studies include electron-beam processing, so they do not establish a same-domain LPBF default. For AlSi10Mg, the unmodified law and its process/mean-stress context are discussed in [Strauß et al. 2024, section 2.3.1 Eq.1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ffe.14441); no specimen hardness or fatigue rows were loaded. The original lineage is Murakami/Endo 1994 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0142-1123(94)90001-9); its original full text was not authenticated in this research.

### F2. Gumbel and F3. CT detection threshold

F2 is a **statistical model**, not an independent physics proof. All five alloys have the same conditional Tier 2 route once comparable inspected **block maxima** are supplied. Model `F(x)=exp(-exp(-(x-mu)/beta))`, `beta>0`; method-of-moments uses `beta=s_std*sqrt(6)/pi`, `mu=mean-gamma*beta`; probability p quantile `x_p=mu-beta*ln(-ln p)`. For n equivalent independent target blocks, `P(max<=x)=F(x)^n`; use the explicit probability and block/target-volume design. See NIST/SEMATECH [Extreme Value Type I, maximum case](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda366g.htm). This is statistical sampling transfer even if the alloy identity matches. The EBM/Ti or Al fatigue source does not validate target-volume extrapolation.

Require independent maxima from stated equal inspected area/volume, sampled region/stress relevance, projected loading direction and comparable detection/censoring. n>=3 permits the implemented two-moment fit only and must not claim adequate tail evidence. A single known defect still supports F1 directly; it does not need a fictional Gumbel fit. Refuse missing sampling design, zero empirical variance, mixed unmatched block sizes, nonpositive predicted physical size, unknown target design or numerical failure. With individual CT pore values but no block design, show the observed maximum with the label **observed in supplied data**, not a fitted population extreme. That measured descriptive number is not an estimate tier and does not become a part-wide maximum. Visible refusal: "A largest-defect distribution needs independent maxima from comparable inspected regions and a target volume. The supplied pore list can show its observed maximum, but cannot establish the part-wide maximum."

F3 `ctDetectionThreshold_um` is instrument/segmentation metadata, not an alloy prediction. For Ti64, AlSi10Mg, 316L and IN718, echo the supplied **measured/validated CT detection threshold** with its method and probability-of-detection definition; no numerical tier is appropriate for an echoed measurement. IN625 is currently unserved. If absent say "Add the CT detection threshold and its validation method to describe which defect sizes may be missed." A voxel size, the minimum observed pore or a Gumbel quantile is not a validated CT detection threshold. A user-assumed threshold may be shown only as an explicitly assumed inspection scenario, never as measured instrument capability.
